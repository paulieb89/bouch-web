# Short-form video production

Use [creative-production](../creative-production/SKILL.md) for the shared workflow and experiment lessons. A short can be animated typography, documentary footage, illustration, product imagery, live action, character-led, still-led or another form that fits the brief. Do not default to component motion, recurring characters or a house style.

## Brief and make a creative choice

- Confirm audience, platform or delivery context, length, aspect ratio, story or message, factual sources, tone and private/public status. Keep source facts and caveats attached to the lines or scenes that need them.
- When the visual language is open, prepare meaningfully different treatments, such as a visual metaphor, a documentary sequence, a designed editorial world, a human performance or a product demonstration. The examples are prompts, not a fixed menu. Ask for the user's choice before committing when the choice materially changes the video.
- Inspect references to the degree claimed. Separate full playback and listening from sampled frames, thumbnails, transcripts and descriptions. Research current production tools and usage terms when they could change the achievable result.
- Choose a workflow for the intended output: examples from past work include editable Remotion scenes with synthesized sound and HTML/SVG scenes captured in a browser and assembled with FFmpeg. Check available tools in the builder's runtime; do not assume the supervisor's access transfers.

## Prove production readiness before the full build

- Match every prominent visual to controllable source material: licensed footage, original shoot, layered artwork, a suitable model or geometry, generated video with permitted use, or another real asset. A flat concept still may communicate color and framing without providing separated layers, reliable character consistency, a rig, or an animatable object.
- Test a representative shot or scene with the actual assets and chosen method. If it cannot meet the visual quality implied by the brief, explain the specific gap and adapt the assets, method or direction before building the full runtime. Do not spend the project drawing a detailed substitute for a real product when that is not the goal.
- Keep source, timeline, scripts, assets, provenance, build steps and versions. Use original or properly licensed material; record source and license evidence.

## Review the actual video

- Render an early draft and inspect representative frames at phone size, pacing across the full cut, safe framing, titles/captions, fact wording, spelling and transitions. Check the complete file technically: expected dimensions, frame rate, duration, codecs, full decode and audio presence.
- Listen to the soundtrack and voice in playback. Human listening is required to judge tone, naturalness, harshness, intelligibility, mix and pacing; loudness or spectral measurements cannot determine whether the sound is good. If no human review has happened, state that plainly.
- Make focused revisions from observed problems or user feedback, then review the revised export. Preserve earlier versions when comparison is useful.
- Close with a concise evidence record: creative direction, chosen tools, asset provenance, technical checks, what was visually reviewed and heard, user feedback, limits, and the next decision. A private pilot or positive experiment does not by itself authorize a recurring series or publication.
