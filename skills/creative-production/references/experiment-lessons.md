# Reusable lessons from BOUCH experiments

These are evidence-backed prompts, not mandatory styles or templates. Check the current project handoff for more detail and current status.

## Websites

- Early visual boards and rendered homepage studies were rated highly (around 8–9/10), but felt too much like company websites. Strong visual polish alone did not make the work feel personal.
- Workbench felt more personal; Loupe was judged a 10/10 creative experiment, but its legal emphasis was too narrow and the magnifier became over-literal. Retain the value of a distinctive interaction and authored identity; do not reuse either concept as the portfolio direction by default.
- Register was considered a very good design and website. Its boxes, borders and shading are a contemporary visual reference to keep in the vocabulary, not a mandated design system.
- The browser-inspired Benchmark study showed that inspectable interactions, source disclosure and fictional-data labels can make a demo tangible. It was also too legally focused and not personal enough. Review both the interaction and the actual subject matter.
- Browser-based checks with Playwright and Chrome DevTools have been exercised in a fresh Claude session for navigation, snapshots, interaction, console and basic layout checks. Availability varies by host; verify in the active agent.

## Short-form video

- The AI explainer was refined in editable Remotion source. Its method—clear explanation, rendered versions, phone-scale review, targeted revision, and a human listening check—was successful and reusable. The stencil look and specific motion are not defaults; the user approved the experiment, not every future use.
- The AI weekly update pilot was watchable with small improvements; the voice passed provisionally, and the user would watch it with a better voice and comparison to similar concepts. This does not approve a weekly series or publication.
- The later controller motion study was rejected. A flat generated reference did not provide a controllable product asset; the agent filled the gap by drawing the controller, which missed the expected quality. Establish asset and production readiness before implementation, then stop or change approach if the gap remains.
- Audio meters and render checks can catch technical problems, but do not establish that a voice sounds natural, the mix feels good, or pacing is engaging. A person needs to watch and listen.
- Past short experiments used different methods, including Remotion/TypeScript with synthesized audio, and HTML/SVG frame capture with FFmpeg and local speech. Treat them as options to evaluate against each new brief, not a required stack.

## Cross-project pattern

The repeatable part is: agree what the audience should experience; explore distinct ideas where the choice matters; secure usable assets and a viable method; make an editable draft; review what it actually looks and sounds like; make specific revisions; verify the result; preserve source, evidence and honest human feedback. The visual treatment should emerge from the project.
